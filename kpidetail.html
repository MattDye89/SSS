2 · Post-I9 hiring and retention challenges

A light look at turnover post i9 resolutions shows increased volatility with staff turnover and increased training attention.

Context · Kitchen output through the disruption and back

Productivity fell for five quarters, then passed where it started.

Items per BOH hour and composite sales per labor hour, Q1 2025 through P6 2026. Output held through Q2 2025, broke when the kitchens turned over, bottomed in Q1 2026 six percent below baseline, and has now recovered past the prior peak. The dip is the cost of losing a workforce and rebuilding it, not a scheduling failure, and the recovery is the crews we have now getting good.

5 · Facilities Scores: Q4 we changed the auditor and raised the bar

The change in Regional Manager in Q4 25 brought a new scorer to the process, with a purposeful increase to clearing the standard bar.

15 · What happened in Q4 2025

Facilities Score Trend

Every single store fell, and the average dropped 11.5 points at once.

Q4 2025 was the first audit executed by our new RM, and the method got more detailed. A simultaneous drop across every location in the system is the signature of a measurement change, not eleven buildings deteriorating in the same ninety days. We also moved the passing bar from 90 to 92 in 2025, deliberately, because everyone was clearing 90 and we wanted the standard to mean something.

16 · Since the new standard took effect

Every location is up. Several by a lot.

LocationQ4 '25 first auditQ1 '26Q2 '26Gain

Ten of ten locations improved against their first score under the new audit, by an average of 6.6 points. CP49 gained more than fifteen. The distance still left to 92 is the distance to a bar we raised on purpose, measured by an auditor we made stricter on purpose. Both were choices, and the stores have closed most of the gap in two quarters.
